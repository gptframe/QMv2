00—07 / 8-state representative route

The requirement enters.
The component moves forward.

A component does not begin at the machine. It begins with a drawing, a material, a quantity and a clear definition of what must be accepted.

The route below is illustrative. Operations are selected to suit the part; not every component passes through every process.

  1. Engineering requirement: drawings, CAD models or a component sample can establish the enquiry.
  2. Material: grade, form and supporting-document requirements are considered against the drawing and application.
  3. Preparation: stock is prepared for the selected manufacturing route.
  4. Machining: operations create the geometry, interfaces and drawing-defined features required by the component.
  5. Secondary operations: turning, tooling, fabrication or other supporting operations are considered where the requirement calls for them.
  6. Inspection: specified characteristics are checked to the scope agreed for the order.
  7. Finished component: the component is prepared for delivery in line with agreed project, packaging and documentation requirements.
  8. Delivery and accountability: manufacturing route, location and delivery terms are defined for the quotation.

Quantamorph engineering / Beyond the component

Parts, tooling
and production systems.

Quantamorph can take a requirement from design and reverse engineering through components, tooling, fixtures, fabrication and special-purpose machines. The route is selected around the job—not forced into a standard process.

Where the requirement extends beyond a single manufactured part, the enquiry can be reviewed around function, interfaces, acceptance criteria and the responsibilities required to take it forward.

  1. 01

    CAD & design development

    Concept design, 2D and 3D modelling, drawing development and manufacturing detail can be included before production begins.

  2. 02

    Special-purpose machines

    Bespoke production or checking equipment can be assessed against the required operation, interfaces, cycle expectations and safety responsibilities.

  3. 03

    Checking fixtures & gauges

    Dedicated checking fixtures, gauges and CMM part-holding fixtures can be scoped around the approved part definition and intended inspection method.

  4. 04

    Application & welding fixtures

    Workholding can be developed around the manufacturing or assembly operation, including application-specific and welding-fixture enquiries.

  5. 05

    Reverse engineering

    Sample-led reconstruction can be reviewed where ownership, design intent, acceptance basis and permitted use of the original component are clear.

  6. 06

    Press tools & conventional toolmaking

    Tools, dies and production aids can be developed using CNC, VMC and conventional manufacturing methods to suit the requirement.

Actual work The gallery uses real component and fixture photographs from the supplied capability material. Detailed scope, validation and delivery requirements are agreed for each RFQ.

Quality / Define before manufacture

Precision means little
until it is verified.

The inspection route should be part of the commercial scope—not an assumption made after production.

  1. 01

    Drawing revision

    Identify the approved requirement and the features that govern acceptance.

  2. 02

    Acceptance criteria

    Clarify specified characteristics, standards and any project-specific conditions.

  3. 03

    Inspection scope

    Agree checking level, equipment needs and any reporting requirement at quotation.

  4. 04

    Release records

    Supply inspection, material or delivery records where they form part of the agreed order.

No universal tolerance, inspection level or documentation package is implied. These are defined for each quotation.
